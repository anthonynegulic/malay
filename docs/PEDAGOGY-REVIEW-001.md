# Bukit — Pedagogy & First-Run UX Review (001)

*10 July 2026 · prepared after the first live QA session, for external input.
Companion doc: `HANDOVER.md` (what's built and why), `BRIEF.md` (original brief).*

---

## 1. Context: what Bukit is and how a lesson works today

Bukit is a personal Malay-learning PWA for a beginner preparing to live in Penang.
Core loop, once per day:

1. **Ulangkaji · review** — FSRS-scheduled flashcards (recognition: Malay → English).
2. **Baca · read** — a short passage generated by Claude, constrained to words the
   learner has studied plus 2–3 new words (underlined) that debut in context. A
   difficulty tier (0–3, keyed to studied-word count) controls length, format,
   gloss visibility, containment strictness, and question language. One typed
   comprehension question, self-marked.
3. **Cakap · speak** — a short written-production task, graded leniently by Claude.
   Tier-ramped: tier 0 = complete Malay pattern with one blank; tier 1 = situation +
   sentence starter; tiers 2–3 = open situation.

New words met today become review cards **tomorrow** (deliberate: the passage is
first *exposure*, the card is first *test*, spaced by a night). No streaks; a weekly
rhythm target and gap-visible history instead.

## 2. The question that prompted this review

> Once you've studied the new words, answered the question and done the speaking
> part — that's the lesson done? Should we be able to go right into reviewing the
> cards we've just learnt if we want?

**Short answer: yes to the instinct, with one adjustment.** Retrieval practice
shortly after first exposure measurably strengthens encoding — every serious SRS
(Anki included) runs same-day "learning steps" before the first day-long interval.
But the FSRS scheduler's first *real* interval should stay at tomorrow; that
overnight gap is what builds long-term retention. The recommended shape:

- End the lesson with a short, **no-stakes recall pass** over today's new words —
  flip through, try to recall, self-check. It primes tomorrow's scheduled review
  without distorting the schedule.
- This also fixes a structural oddity: new words currently only ever appear
  *inside* the passage. There is never a moment where the app plainly says "here
  are your three words today." An explicit close gives the lesson a satisfying
  ending and a clear mental model.

## 3. First-run walkthrough (keen beginner, zero Malay, first open)

**What lands well:** onboarding is one question ("Have you studied Malay before?")
and a brand-new learner is in the app within seconds. The Today screen is calm and
unambiguous. The tier-0 reading — glosses visible, three underlined words, an
English question — is genuinely doable on day one. Navigation (three tabs +
settings) never loses the user.

**The seams, in order of severity:**

1. **Day-0 empty review.** A new user's very first tap of "Mula · start today's
   session" lands on a full-screen *"Tiada kad hari ini — the queue is clear"* —
   an empty state for a system (reviews) the user hasn't met yet, as step one of
   lesson one. Day 0 should route straight into the reading.
2. **New words are never introduced.** They appear mid-sentence, underlined; the
   user must notice them and infer their status. See §2 — an explicit
   intro/practice moment solves this.
3. **Register toggle too early.** The BAKU / TUKAR·SWITCH control in the reading
   header is meaningless jargon on day one — standard vs. colloquial register is a
   distinction a beginner has no context to want. Hide until tier 1.
4. **Word of the day, day 0.** The Today header greets a brand-new user with a
   Malay word they've never seen and no explanation of why it's there. Minor;
   could be suppressed until a few words are studied, or captioned.

## 4. Pedagogy audit — is the approach right?

**The bones are stronger than most commercial apps**, because the two hardest
things are already correct:

- **Comprehensible input at i+1 with enforced containment.** Passages are
  server-validated to stay inside studied vocabulary (tier-controlled tolerance,
  regeneration on failure). This is the thing Duolingo-style apps fundamentally
  don't do, and it's the engine of acquisition here.
- **FSRS scheduling** — current best-in-class spaced-repetition science, with a
  0.90 retention target.

Also defensible and worth keeping: typed retrieval on the comprehension question
(testing effect, honest self-assessment), scaffolded low-anxiety output
(affective-filter-friendly), register awareness as a first-class concept (rare,
and essential for Penang specifically), and no-streak habit design (weekly rhythm,
gaps visible but not punished).

**Gaps, ranked by real-world value for someone moving to Penang:**

| # | Gap | Why it matters | Cheapest good fix |
|---|-----|----------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Listening is the weakest limb** | You'll *hear* fast, colloquial, Utara-flavoured Malay long before you read it. Audio today is one optional TTS button. | Per-line audio in the reading; later, a "listen first, then reveal text" mode at higher tiers. Highest-value addition on the board. |
| 2 | **No same-day retrieval of new words** | First-exposure encoding is weakest exactly when practice is most valuable. | End-of-lesson recall pass (§2). Small build. |
| 3 | **"Speak" is actually typing** | Fine for grammar; does nothing for the mouth. Pronunciation confidence comes from reps. | A shadowing step: hear the model sentence, say it aloud, no grading. (Malay speech recognition is too unreliable to gate on — don't try.) |
| 4 | **Grammar is 100% implicit** | Pure exposure works, but adults accelerate with a whisper of explicitness. | One "notice: *nak* = want to" line per reading, drawn from that day's passage. Not a grammar course. |
| 5 | **Recognition-only cards** | Eventually production vocabulary (EN→MS) is needed for words used constantly. | Deliberately deferred from v1 — keep on the ledger, revisit around tier 2. |

---

## 5. Recommended sequencing

1. **Day-0 flow fix + end-of-lesson word practice** — small, closes the loop the
   owner noticed, fixes the worst first-run moment (§3.1, §3.2, §2).
2. **Listening-first reading** — bigger build, highest learning value (§4.1).
3. **Shadowing on the speaking task** (§4.3).
4. Register-toggle gating and word-of-day polish opportunistically alongside.

*Items 4 and 5 of the gap table are noted for the roadmap, not the next sprint.*
